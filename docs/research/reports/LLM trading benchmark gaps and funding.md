# LLM trading benchmark gaps and funding

_Literature review, 2026-10-13. Re-check quarterly (next: 2027-01-13); the field moves fast._

Seven of GlassBox's integrity features have no counterpart in any 2024-2026 LLM trading benchmark the research notes examined: pre-registered claims, a date-blocked shuffle test, deflated Sharpe, forward calibration of per-call stock-direction probabilities, automated checking of the numbers in rationales against the agent's inputs, an externally anchored tamper-evident ledger, and a placebo strategy run live. The other pieces already exist, each in one or two places. Live evaluation is common (DeepFund, Agent Market Arena, LiveTradeBench, Alpha Arena). CLQT has a hash chain, though it is not externally anchored. OpenPM has a random-agent control, but only in replay. PortBench is the only benchmark with bootstrap confidence intervals. No benchmark combines these features, and none models taxes. This matters because the memorization literature finds pre-cutoff backtests to be contaminated, and the best-known live arena (Alpha Arena) was criticized for having no statistical controls. A forward-only platform that leads with calibration and faithfulness, and offers integrity as proof, would occupy a real and defensible niche. On impact, the strongest design suggested by comparable benchmarks has three parts. The headline is a multi-model Brier/ECE leaderboard on equity-relative questions such as "P(beat SPY over 5 days)". The second axis is the share of numbers in rationales that the inputs do not support. Returns appear only as a secondary column. For funding, the realistic options for an unaffiliated individual are credit programs (Anthropic's External Researcher Access Program, Tinker research grants, Google TRC, Modal, Hugging Face ZeroGPU) and a small set of philanthropic cash funders. Startup programs require a company, and most lab research programs require an academic affiliation. Several claims below come from secondary sources and are marked UNVERIFIED. A feature listed as "absent" means it was absent from the portions of each paper that were read, and several long appendices were truncated.

## Live arenas fixed leakage but kept single-run statistics

The field has moved through three ways of handling look-ahead. The first was to assert there was no leakage: StockBench says its March-June 2025 window "falls after the knowledge cutoff of mainstream LLMs, ensuring no data leakage" ([StockBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02209)). The second was to run live: DeepFund, Agent Market Arena (AMA) and LiveTradeBench all stream data in real time ([DeepFund](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.11065v2); [AMA](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11695); [LiveTradeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628v1)). The third is point-in-time access that is checked in code. CLQT has a "hard TimeGate", although its canary and future-shift probes "were not exercised" ([CLQT](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29771)). OpenPM issues a per-run contamination certificate ([OpenPM](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09988)). The older replay frameworks remain the most widely used. TradingAgents has **110,876 GitHub stars**, and its Q1 2024 window reports Sharpe ratios of **5.6-8.2** with no costs, no repeated runs and no tests ([TradingAgents](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20138)). The memorization literature undercuts results like these. Lopez-Lira et al. find that "LLMs cannot be trusted for economic forecasts during periods covered by their training data" and that "post-cutoff, we observe no recall" ([Memorization Problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14765)). FinCAD's decoding correction cuts in-sample returns by **up to 67.1%** on memorized dates ([FinCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24564)). FINSABER finds that earlier LLM advantages "deteriorate significantly" over a 20-year, 100+ symbol test ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07078)).

The live benchmarks bought leakage control at the cost of statistics. Their windows are short (**24-50 trading days**), each is a single run, and none reports confidence intervals or a random-agent control. AlphaForgeBench documents why this matters: "Even with temperature=0 … LLMs generate completely different trading action sequences" ([AlphaForgeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18481v2)). Most also skip costs. LiveTradeBench, DeepFund, StockBench and PolyBench all exclude fees.

## Seven of GlassBox's integrity features are open gaps

The verdicts below compare GlassBox against everything the notes covered. "Already done by others" means at least one benchmark implements the feature in a substantially equivalent form. "Open gap" means no benchmark examined does. In those cases GlassBox's contribution is real, but the claim rests on absence of evidence within the portions read.

| GlassBox feature | Closest prior work | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Forward-only / live evaluation | DeepFund, AMA, LiveTradeBench, Alpha Arena, PortBench and CLQT live legs | **Already done by others** |
| Point-in-time leakage control | CLQT TimeGate, OpenPM certificate | **Already done by others** (GlassBox avoids the issue entirely by being forward-only) |
| Hash-chained call ledger | CLQT round hash chain, which authors say "does not provide non-repudiation against wholesale replacement and recomputation" ([CLQT](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29771)); OpenPM snapshot hashes | **Hash chain: already done by others.** **External anchoring (public timestamp or transparency log): open gap.** GlassBox does not anchor yet; publishing the ledger head hash weekly is S3 task T13 |
| Pre-registered claims | None found | **Open gap** |
| Placebo strategy | OpenPM Random-N and always-flat, replay only ([OpenPM](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09988)) | **Random baseline already done by others in replay; a placebo running live alongside the agents is an open gap** |
| Date-blocked shuffle (permutation) test | None found; CLQT's bootstrap is still "planned" | **Open gap** |
| Deflated Sharpe | No benchmark. A 2026 integrity paper uses it with a trial ledger ([Gencay](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27734)) | **Open gap among benchmarks; the method itself is not new** |
| Bootstrap CIs (if used) | PortBench ([PortBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27887)) | **Already done by others** |
| Per-call P(beat SPY over 5 days) with Brier/reliability | None for stocks. Event forecasting does this: KalshiBench, Prophet Arena ([KalshiBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16030)); PolyBench rejects Brier | **Open gap** for equity-relative forward calibration |
| Audit of numbers in rationales against the logged inputs | CLQT checks rationale-*action* coherence; OpenPM checks manually; Xue's "claim overreach" proxy on replayed trajectories ([arXiv 2605.28850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28850), details UNVERIFIED) | **Open gap** for automated numeric grounding of live rationales |
| Transaction costs | CLQT, PortBench, OpenPM, MarketSenseAI | **Already done by others** |
| Taxes | None found | **Open gap.** GlassBox's paper accounts already model it (realized short/long-term gains, estimated tax, after-tax and liquidation returns) |

Two caveats should shape how GlassBox presents these features. First, deflated Sharpe is weaker evidence than it looks. The same integrity paper shows a leaky oracle with a Sharpe of about **35** that "survives Deflated Sharpe and probability-of-backtest-overfitting testing completely" ([Gencay](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27734)). This argues for presenting the placebo strategy and the shuffle test as the main defenses and deflated Sharpe as a supporting statistic. Second, the rationale audit has reusable building blocks. FinGround's atomic-claim and arithmetic re-computation approach ([FinGround](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23588)) and rule-based numeric matchers ([Hallucination Snowball](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14588)) both exist. GlassBox's novelty lies in applying them to forward trading rationales against the exact inputs logged for each call, not in the matching technique. Being forward-only has one residual risk: news retrieved by tools may itself be misdated or contaminated (an inference stated in the notes).

## A calibration-first leaderboard has the best adoption evidence

Comparable benchmarks reach two audiences in different ways. Calibration and forecasting benchmarks get peer review: ForecastBench (ICLR 2025), Prophet Arena (ICLR 2026) and FutureX (ICLR 2026) all appeared at ICLR ([ForecastBench](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ea74e45a229dac70b5b63b28d8934db6-Abstract-Conference.html); [Prophet Arena](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10009102); [FutureX](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10006496)). Return-based arenas get media reach without rigor. Alpha Arena drew mainstream press and a Musk promotion of Grok 4.20, along with explicit criticism of its short duration and absent statistical controls ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/amp); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/elon-musks-grok-4-20-123855766.html)). Neither type has reached frontier-lab model cards. The finance benchmarks labs do cite are agentic task benchmarks: Vals Finance Agent and Vending-Bench, both UNVERIFIED secondary claims ([Vals](https://www.vals.ai/models/anthropic_claude-opus-5); [Andon Labs](https://andonlabs.com/evals/vending-bench-arena)). The most-adopted benchmarks share a single headline number, a public leaderboard, cheap automatic scoring, and early lab involvement. LMArena grew into a **$1.7B-valued company** ([VKTR](https://www.vktr.com/ai-news/lmarena-raises-150m-series-a-at-17b-valuation/)). SWE-bench Verified "became a standard metric" once OpenAI co-built it, and OpenAI dropped it in February 2026 over contamination ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/)). Open submission plus prizes drives participation: Metaculus drew 34 and then 54 bot makers per quarter at $30k ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Surnjh8A4WjgtQTkZ/q2-ai-benchmark-results-pros-maintain-clear-lead)).

| Design option | Occupancy | Evidence | Fit for GlassBox |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Forward calibration on equity-relative questions | Generic event forecasting is crowded; **no equity-cross-section benchmark found** | KalshiBench, FutureX, Prophet Arena all use events or prediction markets | **Headline metric** |
| B. Rationale numeric-faithfulness score | **Least occupied** | Closest: FAITH (masked numbers in reports), summarization hallucination rates of 4-6.5% ([arXiv 2404.06162](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.06162)) | **Second axis**; closest to the analyst-work framing labs already report |
| C. Live trading P&L leaderboard | Crowded, credibility-challenged | Alpha Arena, LiveTradeBench, AI-Trader | Secondary column only |
| D. Integrity tooling | Emerging, no standard | Gencay 2026; a Manifund look-ahead audit proposal ([Manifund](https://manifund.org/projects/pit-audit-lookahead-bias-audits-for-ai-trading--forecasting-benchmarks)) | Credibility layer, not the draw |

The recommendation follows the Prophet Arena pattern: lead with Brier/ECE, and show returns as a secondary column ([Prophet Arena](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/aec5e2847c5ae90f939ab786774856cc-Paper-Conference.pdf)). Add a second axis for unsupported numbers in rationales. Base the leaderboard on a market base rate (the SPY base rate or analyst consensus), refresh it frequently, accept open submissions, and target a datasets-track paper. One risk is that forecasting scores cluster tightly. Prophet Arena's top five sit within **0.0013** on 1 − Brier ([leaderboard](https://www.prophetarena.co/leaderboard/forecast)), which weakens the leaderboard as a marketing story. The faithfulness axis is where models are more likely to spread apart (an inference). A notes gap remains: StockBench, InvestorBench and FinSearchComp-style finance-forecasting papers were not searched for an equity calibration benchmark, so one could exist.

### Immigration check before company or cash routes

The owner is on an H-1B visa. This is a pointer, not legal advice: before applying to any program that requires forming a company (NVIDIA Inception, AWS Activate, Google for Startups, Microsoft for Startups) or that pays cash to an individual (Emergent Ventures, Manifund, LTFF, Coefficient Giving and similar), the owner should check with an immigration attorney how it interacts with H-1B status, and with a tax professional on the tax treatment. Credit-only programs that require no company avoid the payment question raised in the notes, but whether they raise any status issue is also a question for the attorney, not something these notes establish.

## Conclusion

GlassBox's novelty does not lie in live trading or hashing, which others already do. It lies in turning integrity into falsifiable commitments. Pre-registration, a live placebo and a date-blocked shuffle test let a reader distinguish "the model has skill" from "we found a lucky window". No benchmark examined can offer that, and it is exactly the criticism leveled at Alpha Arena. The evidence also implies a different framing for the product. A benchmark that presents P&L as its headline enters a crowded and distrusted category. One that presents forward calibration and rationale faithfulness, across many models, sits where peer review and lab interest are both pointing, and uses its integrity layer as the reason to believe the numbers.

Three things should be verified before making public claims. The first is the full appendices of CLQT, PortBench and LiveTradeBench, since an absence claim rests on them. The second is Xue's 2605.28850 metric, which is the closest work on rationale faithfulness. The third is whether an equity-specific calibration benchmark exists that the notes missed. Funding should start with credits that need neither a company nor an affiliation, with any cash or incorporation route held until the attorney check above.
